The three tests

Sequence, number and visual memory

Each game stresses working memory in a different way.

The games

  • Sequence: tiles light up in order. Repeat the order exactly. Each level adds one more step.
  • Number: a number flashes, then disappears. Type it back. Each level adds one digit.
  • Visual: a pattern of tiles lights up on a grid that grows over time. Tap all of them.

What is short-term memory?

These games exercise working memory, the small space where you hold information for a few seconds. George Miller's 1956 paper popularised the "seven plus or minus two" idea. Later work, including Nelson Cowan's 2001 review, suggests about four chunks for unrelated items.

Casual tests, not a clinical assessment. Scores vary with attention and tiredness.

How it works

How scoring works

Simple rules, same in every game.

A game

  1. Pick Sequence, Number or Visual.
  2. Click the box. Level 1 starts straight away.
  3. Watch carefully. The game tells you when it is your turn.
  4. A mistake costs one of your three lives and repeats the level. When the lives are gone the game ends and your score is the highest level you completed.

What affects your memory score?

  • Chunking: group digits in twos or threes.
  • Distraction: play when you are rested and undisturbed.
  • Rehearsal: repeat the items quietly as they appear.
  • Strategy: link tiles to a shape or path.
Practice

How to remember more

A few simple techniques make a big difference.

Techniques

  • Chunk digits into groups of two or three.
  • Say the items quietly or in your head as they appear.
  • Link tiles to a shape or path, such as a letter or a snake.

Habits

  • Short sessions beat marathons.
  • Play when you are rested and not distracted.
  • Compare your own best over weeks, not against others.

Memory test FAQ

Answers about scoring and short-term memory.

What does the memory test measure?

Short-term, or working, memory: how much you can hold and reproduce after a few seconds.

How many digits can people remember?

Many people manage about 7 digits, and chunking can push that higher. The common figure comes from Miller's 1956 "seven plus or minus two", though later work suggests about four chunks.

Why do I lose lives?

Any wrong tile, wrong digit or wrong order loses a life. The level then repeats. After three lives the game is over.

Is the sequence the same each time?

A failed level in Sequence mode repeats the same order so you can learn it. Number and Visual make a new pattern.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. Tap the tiles and use your numeric keyboard for numbers.

Are results saved?

Your best level and history are stored in your browser only.
